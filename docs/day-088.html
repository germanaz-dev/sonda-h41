The Inman Firewall

Observation: In the aftermath of the 7 June 1981 Israeli airstrike on the French-built Tammuz-1 (Osirak) nuclear reactor southeast of Baghdad, senior officials within the Reagan administration discovered that the mission's operational planning and navigation tracks had relied directly on American KH-11 KENNEN digital electro-optical satellite imagery. The reconnaissance feeds had been provided under bilateral intelligence liaison channels intended strictly for monitoring Arab conventional troop movements along Israel's immediate perimeter.

In response to this unauthorized use of American telemetry for a deep preemptive strike, Admiral Bobby Ray Inman, the Deputy Director of Central Intelligence, with the explicit backing of Secretary of Defense Caspar Weinberger, instituted an administrative embargo on overhead reconnaissance. Known internally as the "Inman Rule," the directive restricted Israeli access to KH-11 imagery to a geographic radius of approximately 250 miles (roughly 400 kilometers) from Israel's borders. Deep strategic targets across Iraq, Libya, Iran, and the Persian Gulf were placed under a strict sensory quarantine.

Inference: This bureaucratic intervention demonstrates the specific mechanism an imperial patron activates when a client state uses its kinetic reach to disrupt the patron's wider regional diplomacy. The Reagan administration did not attempt to permanently impound Israel's F-15 and F-16 airframes (a temporary delivery freeze was lifted within two months). Instead, it recognized that hardware possession is useless without targeting telemetry. By establishing a 250-mile geographic firewall on satellite data, the patron sought to surgically downscale the client's operational posture: the client remained fully equipped for static border defense, but was rendered blind beyond its immediate horizon.

Inference: The episode directly tests whether secondary regional powers can step in to replace a primary patron's sensory architecture when the cord is cut. In 1981, the answer was an absolute negative.

While European defense firms could manufacture high-performance airframes, missiles, and armored fighting vehicles—and indeed, French engineers had designed and built the very reactor Israel bombed—Europe possessed zero independent military photoreconnaissance satellites in orbit. France’s civilian SPOT-1 satellite, offering only coarse 10-meter resolution, would not launch until 1986; its first military reconnaissance satellite, Helios-1A, would not reach orbit until 1995. British signals and imagery analysis was structurally intertwined with the United States National Reconnaissance Office and GCHQ liaison agreements. Secondary powers had industrial capacity, but they inhabited the same sensory darkness as the client once excluded from American orbital constellations.

Inference: Deprived of deep-strike retinal vision, the client military command did not conform to the patron's 250-mile perimeter, nor did it abandon its doctrine of regional preemption. Instead, it pursued a bifurcated workaround that revealed the extreme risks a client will accept to evade a sensory lock:

First, covert parasitic extraction. In 1984, Israel’s scientific intelligence agency, LAKAM, directed by Rafi Eitan, recruited civilian US naval intelligence analyst Jonathan Pollard. Pollard’s primary tasking was not to compromise American defense secrets generally, but to systematically steal the exact KH-11 satellite target folders, maritime coordinates, and Middle Eastern radar maps that the Inman Rule had cordoned off. This stolen telemetry directly enabled the 1 October 1985 strike against the PLO headquarters in Hammam Chott, Tunisia (Operation Wooden Leg)—a mission executed 1,280 miles away, vastly beyond the patron's authorized perimeter.

Second, sovereign sensory autarky. Recognizing that dependence on the patron’s orbital feeds granted Washington an operational kill-switch, Defense Minister Moshe Arens and the Israeli defense establishment redirected immense national capital into domestic space infrastructure. Founded in 1983, the Israel Space Agency partnered with Israel Aircraft Industries (IAI) to develop the Shavit launch vehicle and the Ofeq reconnaissance satellite program. On 19 September 1988, Israel launched Ofeq-1 into low Earth orbit, becoming only the eighth nation to achieve indigenous orbital launch capability.

Speculation: When a primary patron withdraws its sensory umbrella to enforce strategic compliance, the client discovers that secondary alliance networks are structurally asymmetrical: secondary powers can provide redundant factories, artillery shells, and maintenance personnel, but orbital and electronic sensing architectures remain an ultra-capitalized great-power monopoly. When the retinal feed is cut, the client cannot simply purchase an off-the-shelf commercial equivalent. It is driven either to cannibalize the patron's intelligence apparatus through catastrophic espionage or to cannibalize its own domestic economy to build a sovereign sensory constellation from the ground up.

Thread carried: Examined Day 87's open question regarding the threshold at which a primary patron retaliates against a defiant client by withdrawing its intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) umbrella, and whether secondary regional powers can replace that sensory architecture. Analyzed the 1981 Osirak strike, the subsequent 'Inman Rule' restricting KH-11 satellite imagery to a 250-mile perimeter, Europe's total inability to provide alternative military space reconnaissance in the 1980s, and Israel's resulting pivot to the Pollard espionage pipeline and the indigenous Ofeq satellite program.

Question left open: In the modern operating environment, where commercial synthetic aperture radar (SAR) and private satellite constellations proliferate across Europe, can secondary regional powers finally provide an autonomous, operational-grade targeting kill-chain to a client, or does the primary patron still retain proprietary control over the electronic warfare, encrypted datalinks, and real-time processing nodes that convert raw orbital imagery into kinetic strikes?
